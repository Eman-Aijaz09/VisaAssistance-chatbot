Of course, your children can also live with you in Germany. You can find out here how to bring them to you.
If you or your spouse are entitled to live in Germany, your children under the age of 18 are also entitled to a temporary or permanent residence permit.
If your children are citizens of a member state of the European Union (EU), European Economic Area (EEA) or Switzerland, they do not require a visa to enter Germany and can live here with their parents.
If your children are citizens of a country which is not in the EU, the EEA or Switzerland, they will generally need a visa to enter Germany. If you and your spouse hold temporary resident permits for Germany, your children under 16 years of age will also receive a temporary resident permit in order for them to join you (Section 32 of the Residence Act [AufenthG] (External link) ).
Nationals of Andorra, Australia, Brazil, El Salvador, Honduras, Israel, Japan, Canada, Monaco, New Zealand, San Marino, the Republic of Korea, the United Kingdom of Great Britain and Northern Ireland, and the United States of America who come to Germany for the purpose of family reunification may enter Germany without a visa and then apply for the required residence permit directly at the competent (External link) (External link) foreigners authority (External link) .
The following criteria must be met for a family reunification visa for children:
- The child must not be married, divorced or widowed
- The child must be under 18 years of age.
The visa for family reunification with children can be applied for online via the Consular Services Portal (External link) of the Federal Foreign Office or directly at the German embassy. A list of all missions of the Federal Republic of Germany abroad can be found under Advisory and Contact Services.
Entering Germany with only one parent entitled to custody
If you wish to apply for a visa for your child as a single parent, you will need to obtain the consent of the other parent entitled to custody.
Special requirements apply to children over the age of 16. Your local German Embassy will be able to inform you about them. If your child is already 18 years of age, they must generally apply for a separate resident title of their own.
A child acquires German citizenship at birth if at least one parent holds German citizenship. This is because the principle of descent (jus sanguinis) generally applies in Germany.
Children of foreign parents who are born in Germany may also acquire German citizenship at birth if one parent:
- has been lawfully and habitually resident in Germany for at least five years, and
- holds a permanent right of residence at the time of the child’s birth (e.g. a settlement permit or EU long-term residence permit), or, as a Swiss national or a family member thereof, holds a certificate or residence card confirming lawful residence in Germany under the Agreement on the Free Movement of Persons between the EU and Switzerland.
In such cases, under German law, children acquire German citizenship in addition to their parents’ nationality.
